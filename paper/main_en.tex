\title{KCH-MedRank: Knowledge-Constrained Learning-to-Rank for Evidence-Grounded Medical Retrieval-Augmented Generation}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Medical retrieval-augmented generation systems require evidence selection that is accurate, medically grounded, and inspectable before answer generation. This paper presents KCH-MedRank, a knowledge-constrained learning-to-rank framework for biomedical evidence retrieval and reranking, with local graph and hypergraph features used as interpretable structural signals. Enhanced candidate generation improves the top-100 evidence ceiling, and KCH-MedRank improves top-10 evidence retrieval over strong Hybrid RRF on the held-out BioASQ test split. Against a true MedCPT Cross-Encoder baseline on the same candidate pool, KCH-MedRank significantly improves Recall@10, while MRR@10 and nDCG@10 remain positive but non-significant. A key flat-feature ablation shows that much of the gain is already captured by retrieval, biomedical semantic, MeSH, entity, and relation features without any graph structure. Adding medical knowledge constraints to the local hypergraph reverses the noise introduced by structure-only hypergraph features and yields statistically significant gains over the no-medical-knowledge hypergraph variant ($p < 0.01$). However, the full model is not significantly better than the flat knowledge LTR baseline at $k{=}10$, and the hypergraph architecture provides only modest early-rank benefit over pairwise graph decomposition at $k{=}5$. A controlled timing comparison shows that KCH-MedRank uses a precomputed dense biomedical semantic feature and runs 18.6$\times$ faster than MedCPT Cross-Encoder at the reranking stage. An external BEIR NFCorpus retrieval-only diagnostic provides an additional robustness check for supervised reranking. The results position KCH-MedRank as a white-box evidence selection module for medical RAG rather than as a clinical decision system.
\end{abstract}
