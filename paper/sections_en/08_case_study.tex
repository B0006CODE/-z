\section{Case Study}

The failure analysis compares Enhanced Hybrid w122 with Enhanced KCH-MedRank at top 10 over 943 paired held-out BioASQ questions. KCH-MedRank rescues 661 gold passages into top 10 and loses 307 gold passages from top 10. This net gain supports the retrieval claim, but the lost cases are important because they show where knowledge-constrained features can overrule a strong lexical or semantic rank.

One rescued pattern is illustrated by the question ``What colonoscopy findings have been reported in autism''. The gold passage was ranked 35th by Hybrid RRF and 10th by the hypergraph reranker. The passage shares both query MeSH concepts, \emph{colon} and \emph{colonoscopy}, with the question, while the local graph contains document-MeSH and shared-entity connections among neighboring candidates. This is the type of case where exact lexical ranking alone can miss a medically aligned passage, especially when the passage language or wording differs from the question.

Another rescued example asks whether phylogenetic analysis exists for HIV. The gold passage moves from rank 32 to rank 5, supported by the direct entity overlap on HIV and by local hypergraph connectivity among HIV-related candidates. This example is useful because the gain is not driven by PrimeKG relations; it comes from entity and local candidate structure, consistent with the feature-importance analysis that treats PrimeKG as auxiliary.

Lost cases are also informative. For the question about proteins undergoing both mono-ubiquitination and poly-ubiquitination, a gold passage drops from rank 2 to rank 18. The question contains MeSH-like signals related to ubiquitin and ubiquitination, but the passage uses related surface forms and broader post-translational-processing concepts. This suggests that hierarchy and synonym handling still fail for some biochemical terminology. Similar losses occur for thyroid hormone analogs, cilengitide receptors, dasatinib treatment, GIST treatment, and Fanconi anemia. These examples motivate future improvements in biomedical concept normalization rather than stronger claims about the current relation features.
