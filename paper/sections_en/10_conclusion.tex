\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented KCH-MedRank, an interpretable medical evidence control layer implemented as a lightweight and reproducible knowledge-constrained local hypergraph learning-to-rank framework. The method keeps first-stage retrieval practical, constructs a local cross-granularity hypergraph over question, passage, document, entity, MeSH, and relation signals, and uses the resulting medical and structural signals as interpretable learning-to-rank features.

The BioASQ results show stronger top-10 evidence retrieval than original and enhanced Hybrid RRF, with statistically significant improvements across all top-10 retrieval metrics against enhanced Hybrid RRF. Against a true MedCPT Cross-Encoder baseline on the same enhanced candidate pool, the strongest supported gain is statistically significant Recall@10 improvement; MRR@10 and nDCG@10 remain positive but non-significant trends. A controlled reranking-stage timing comparison further shows that KCH-MedRank uses a precomputed dense biomedical semantic feature rather than a Cross-Encoder feature and is 18.6$\times$ faster than MedCPT Cross-Encoder on the held-out test pool when candidate generation, one-time model loading, and offline training are excluded.

A structural ablation provides the central evidence for the medical knowledge contribution. The local hypergraph alone, without medical knowledge annotations, introduces structural noise and underperforms the semantic-only LambdaMART baseline. Adding biomedical entity features, exact and hierarchy-aware MeSH features, and auxiliary relation features reverses this effect across all top-10 metrics: the Knowledge$\times$Structure interaction is statistically significant (Recall@10 $p = 0.003$, MRR@10 $p = 0.004$, nDCG@10 $p = 0.0002$). This demonstrates that medical knowledge constraints are necessary for the hypergraph to contribute positively, and that the proposed integration of knowledge and structure produces a net ranking benefit beyond either component alone.

An external BEIR NFCorpus retrieval-only diagnostic supports the robustness of supervised retrieval-feature reranking, while also showing that the full medical knowledge feature stack should not be claimed to transfer without dataset-specific construction.

The appendix PubMedQA Qwen3-8B pilot indicates that evidence ordering can affect downstream answer selection under a fixed prompt and decoding setup, but it remains a limited diagnostic rather than a primary generation claim. Overall, the results support KCH-MedRank as a white-box evidence selection and grounding module for evidence-grounded medical QA. The method should not be viewed as a clinically validated answer generation or decision-support system.
