\title{KCH-MedRank: Knowledge-Constrained Learning-to-Rank for Evidence-Grounded Medical Retrieval-Augmented Generation}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Biomedical retrievers routinely place gold evidence passages inside the candidate pool and then fail to pull them into the top 10. The passages are not irrelevant. Pairwise query-passage scoring simply has no way to see the higher-order medical relations that connect them. We introduce KCH-MedRank, a knowledge-constrained learning-to-rank framework that gives the ranker access to retrieval scores, MeSH hierarchy signals, biomedical entity overlap, and an optional local hypergraph. The central finding is that flat biomedical knowledge features already carry most of the gain. Hypergraph structure helps, but only when grounded in domain constraints; strip out MeSH and entity annotations and raw hypergraph topology hurts ranking rather than helping it. On BioASQ, KCH-MedRank beats a true MedCPT Cross-Encoder baseline on Recall@10 ($+0.0157$, $p=0.0076$) and runs $18.6\times$ faster at reranking time. Interpretability analysis traces all 648 rescued passages to three knowledge-grounded mechanisms---MeSH hierarchy paths account for 75.9\% of them.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{5pt}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Biomedical evidence retrieval; learning-to-rank; medical knowledge graph; MeSH hierarchy; hypergraph diffusion; interpretable reranking
