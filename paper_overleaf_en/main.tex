\title{KCH-MedRank: Knowledge-Constrained Learning-to-Rank for Evidence-Grounded Medical Retrieval-Augmented Generation}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Biomedical evidence retrieval for medical QA fails when ranking relies on pairwise similarity alone---gold passages connected through MeSH hierarchy paths or shared biomedical entities are scattered outside the top-10 window. We introduce KCH-MedRank, a knowledge-constrained learning-to-rank framework that enriches each query's candidate neighborhood with interpretable features drawn from retrieval scores, MeSH hierarchy-aware signals, biomedical entities, and optional local hypergraph structure. On the BioASQ benchmark, KCH-MedRank significantly improves Recall@10 over a true MedCPT Cross-Encoder baseline ($+0.0157$, $p=0.0076$) while running $18.6\times$ faster at reranking time. Systematic ablations show that flat biomedical knowledge features account for most aggregate improvement, while domain constraints are required for hypergraph structure to contribute signal rather than noise. Interpretability analysis traces 648 rescued passages to MeSH hierarchy paths (75.9\%), shared entity clusters, and PrimeKG relations.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{5pt}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Biomedical evidence retrieval; learning-to-rank; medical knowledge graph; MeSH hierarchy; hypergraph diffusion; interpretable reranking
