\title{KCH-MedRank: Knowledge-Constrained Learning-to-Rank for Evidence-Grounded Medical Retrieval-Augmented Generation}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Biomedical evidence retrieval for medical QA fails when ranking relies on pairwise similarity alone---gold passages connected through MeSH hierarchy paths or shared biomedical entities are scattered outside the top-10 window. We introduce KCH-MedRank, a knowledge-constrained learning-to-rank framework that enriches each query's candidate neighborhood with interpretable features drawn from retrieval scores, MeSH hierarchy-aware signals, biomedical entities, and optional local hypergraph structure. On the BioASQ benchmark, KCH-MedRank significantly improves Recall@10 over a true MedCPT Cross-Encoder baseline ($+0.0157$, $p=0.0076$) while running $18.6\times$ faster at reranking time. Structural ablations reveal two key findings: flat biomedical knowledge features already capture most of the gain, and medical knowledge constraints are necessary for hypergraph structure to be useful---without them, raw topology introduces noise and degrades ranking. A three-mechanism interpretability analysis traces rescues to MeSH hierarchy paths (75.9\%), shared entity clusters, and PrimeKG relations, confirming that the reranker's decisions follow medically grounded pathways.
\end{abstract}
