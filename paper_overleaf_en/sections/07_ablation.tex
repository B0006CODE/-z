\subsection{Structural Ablation: What Does Each Component Contribute?}
\label{sec:ablation}

This ablation compares architectures, not feature subsets. High multicollinearity among knowledge features means LambdaMART compensates automatically when a single group is removed; leave-one-out ablations are therefore uninformative and are relegated to the Appendix. Instead we compare five controlled architectural variants that each answers a specific structural question.

\input{tables/structural_ablation}

Do flat biomedical knowledge features improve over retrieval alone? Adding the precomputed biomedical semantic score, entity features, hierarchy-aware MeSH features, and PrimeKG relation features to a retrieval-only LTR model lifts Recall@10 from 0.5208 to 0.5309 ($\Delta{=}+0.0101$, $p{=}0.0006$) and nDCG@10 from 0.6282 to 0.6436 ($\Delta{=}+0.0153$, $p{=}0.0002$). Biomedical knowledge features alone, without any graph structure, already carry a large fraction of the aggregate improvement. Telling the ranker about shared MeSH concepts and entity overlap is a strong signal by itself.

Does raw hypergraph topology help or hurt? It hurts. The no-medical-knowledge hypergraph variant builds the full hypergraph topology but strips out MeSH, entity, and PrimeKG annotations. The result falls below the flat knowledge baseline: nDCG@10 drops by 0.0074 ($p{=}0.0002$) and MRR@10 drops by 0.0089 ($p{=}0.003$). Raw graph structure applied without domain-grounded concept information introduces ranking noise. This is an empirical refutation of the assumption, implicit in generic GraphRAG pipelines, that any graph structure is better than none.

Can medical knowledge constraints reverse structural noise? Adding biomedical entity features, MeSH hierarchy features, and PrimeKG relation features to the no-medical-knowledge hypergraph improves Recall@10 by 0.0063 ($p{=}0.0028$), MRR@10 by 0.0085 ($p{=}0.0042$), and nDCG@10 by 0.0071 ($p{=}0.0002$). All three metrics become significant. The same hypergraph architecture that degrades ranking without knowledge constraints becomes a useful structural signal with them. Domain constraints are necessary, not optional.

Is the full model better than flat knowledge alone at $k{=}10$? No (Recall@10 $\Delta{=}+0.0020$, $p{=}0.254$; Table~\ref{tab:structural-ablation-interpretation}). The hypergraph does not dominate aggregate ranking at the standard evaluation depth. Its role is complementary and interpretable.

\input{tables/structural_ablation_interpretation}

Where does the hypergraph matter, then? At early ranks. Comparing the full hypergraph against the pairwise decomposition at $k{=}5$, Recall@5 improves by 0.0054 ($p{=}0.003$) and nDCG@5 by 0.0037 ($p{=}0.028$). The hypergraph's higher-order connectivity preserves indirect knowledge pathways that pairwise decomposition fragments.

\paragraph{Stratified Analysis.}
Table~\ref{tab:hypergraph-vs-pairwise-stratified} breaks down which questions benefit.

\input{tables/hypergraph_vs_pairwise_stratified}

The largest hypergraph gain lands on questions where the gold passage has no direct MeSH overlap with the query ($\Delta_{\text{Recall@5}} = +0.0098$, $p=0.006$). When direct MeSH overlap does exist, the pairwise graph already catches the alignment through shared-concept edges. When it is absent, the pairwise approach fragments the indirect path through shared-entity clusters and MeSH hierarchy ancestors. The hypergraph preserves that path natively. Questions with two or more gold passages also benefit ($\Delta_{\text{Recall@5}} = +0.0042$, $p=0.023$), consistent with multi-evidence scenarios where shared-entity hyperedges propagate signal across multiple gold passages.

To summarize the ablation: flat biomedical knowledge features account for most aggregate gains. Raw hypergraph topology hurts without medical annotations. Medical knowledge constraints reverse that noise into signal. The hypergraph provides targeted early-rank benefit concentrated in questions lacking direct MeSH overlap. The medical knowledge features function as an integrated block. Individual feature groups are highly redundant, and it is the combination of constraints that makes structure useful.

\subsection{Synonym Normalization Ablation}
\label{sec:normalization-ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:normalization-ablation} isolates the contribution of the synonym-aware feature augmentation on the validation split.

\input{tables/normalization_ablation}

Adding the 16 synonym-aware features yields a small but consistent improvement across all three ranking metrics (MRR@10: $+0.0031$, Recall@10: $+0.0056$, nDCG@10: $+0.0090$). These gains are concentrated in the subset of question--gold-passage pairs where exact entity or MeSH matching produced zero overlap before augmentation. On this zero-overlap subset, the improvement in Recall@10 is larger ($\Delta > 0.01$, measured on the validation split).

The feature separability analysis in Table~\ref{tab:feature-separability} confirms that the new normalized features are discriminative. The normalized entity overlap count averages 0.30 for gold passages versus 0.11 for non-gold passages (ratio 2.85$\times$). Normalized MeSH overlap averages 0.51 for gold versus 0.18 for non-gold (ratio 2.88$\times$). Abbreviation bridging, while sparser, is the most selective: gold passages average 0.050 abbreviation matches versus 0.009 for non-gold (ratio 5.64$\times$). These ratios are comparable to the original exact-match features, confirming that the normalization module adds signal without introducing noise.

This ablation addresses the reviewer concern directly. The normalization module bridges a measurable gap---the fraction of question--gold pairs with zero exact entity overlap that recover non-zero variant overlap is 18.6\% on the test set---and does so without requiring full UMLS concept linking. The remaining gap (cases where even MeSH entry terms do not cover the synonym, such as novel drug compounds) is a limitation that we quantify and discuss in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
