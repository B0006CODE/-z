\section{Conclusion}

We have presented KCH-MedRank, a knowledge-constrained learning-to-rank framework for biomedical evidence retrieval and reranking. The method enriches each query's candidate neighborhood with retrieval scores, biomedical semantic features, MeSH hierarchy-aware features, entity and relation features, and optional local hypergraph structure. A flat knowledge LTR baseline and a pairwise graph decomposition isolate the contribution of each component, showing that flat biomedical knowledge features account for most aggregate gains while the hypergraph contributes targeted early-rank benefits ($k{=}5$) in questions lacking direct concept overlap.

On BioASQ, KCH-MedRank achieves statistically significant top-10 gains over enhanced Hybrid RRF across all retrieval metrics and significantly improves Recall@10 over a true MedCPT Cross-Encoder baseline. The reranker runs $18.6\times$ faster than the cross-encoder during reranking by using precomputed dense biomedical semantic features. Structural ablation reveals the central finding: medical knowledge constraints are necessary for hypergraph structure to provide useful signal---without them, raw topology introduces noise and degrades ranking. A three-mechanism interpretation analysis traces 648 rescued gold passages to MeSH hierarchy paths (75.9\%), shared entity clusters, and PrimeKG relations, providing quantitative evidence that the reranker's decisions follow medically grounded pathways.

BEIR NFCorpus confirms the robustness of supervised retrieval-feature reranking. The PubMedQA Qwen3-8B pilot in the appendix indicates that evidence ordering affects downstream answer selection under fixed conditions, motivating full-scale generation studies. Taken together, KCH-MedRank offers an effective, interpretable, and efficient white-box evidence selection module for evidence-grounded medical QA.
