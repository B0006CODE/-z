\section{Ablation Study}

This section analyzes the contributions of flat biomedical knowledge features, graph structure, hypergraph structure, and medical knowledge constraints through controlled architecture comparisons rather than single-feature-group removal. As noted in Section~5, leave-one-feature-group ablations are retained in the appendix as complementary feature-coverage diagnostics, but are not used as the primary ablation evidence because high multicollinearity among knowledge features leads to automatic compensation.

\subsection{Flat Biomedical Knowledge Features Provide a Strong Gain}

Comparison (1) in Table~\ref{tab:structural-ablation} isolates the contribution of non-structural biomedical features above retrieval signals alone. The flat knowledge LTR baseline adds the precomputed biomedical semantic score, entity features, exact and hierarchy-aware MeSH features, and auxiliary PrimeKG relation features, but removes all graph and hypergraph diffusion, centrality, local edge-count, and interaction features. This improves Recall@10 from 0.5208 to 0.5309 ($\Delta = +0.0101$, $p = 0.0006$) and nDCG@10 from 0.6282 to 0.6436 ($\Delta = +0.0153$, $p = 0.0002$). MRR@10 improves from 0.7791 to 0.7872 ($\Delta = +0.0080$, $p = 0.029$). These results directly address the flat-feature alternative explanation: much of the aggregate ranking gain can be obtained from retrieval, biomedical semantic, and medical knowledge features without explicit graph construction.

\subsection{The Hypergraph Alone Introduces Noise}

Comparison (2) isolates the effect of the local hypergraph structure without medical knowledge constraints. This variant builds the hypergraph topology but removes biomedical MeSH, entity, and PrimeKG feature information. The resulting model underperforms the flat knowledge LTR baseline: nDCG@10 drops by 0.0074 ($p = 0.0002$) and MRR@10 drops by 0.0089 ($p = 0.003$). Recall@10 drops by 0.0044 (negative trend). This indicates that raw structure does not substitute for biomedical knowledge features; without medically grounded concept annotations, the hypergraph introduces structural noise that degrades ranking quality.

\subsection{Medical Knowledge Constraints Transform the Hypergraph from Noise to Signal}

Comparison (3) is the central structural ablation. It compares KCH-MedRank against the same hypergraph architecture with all medical knowledge features removed. Adding biomedical entity features, exact MeSH features, MeSH hierarchy features, and PrimeKG relation features improves Recall@10 by 0.0063 ($p = 0.0028$), MRR@10 by 0.0085 ($p = 0.0042$), and nDCG@10 by 0.0071 ($p = 0.0002$). All three top-10 metrics are statistically significant.

Figure~\ref{fig:structural-ablation-deltas} summarizes the interpretation visually. The hypergraph structure alone degrades ranking, while medical knowledge constraints reverse this degradation inside the hypergraph variant. However, Comparison (4) shows that the full model is not significantly better than the flat knowledge LTR baseline at $k{=}10$ (Recall@10 $\Delta=+0.0020$, $p=0.254$; MRR@10 $\Delta=-0.0004$, $p=0.864$; nDCG@10 $\Delta=-0.0003$, $p=0.816$). Therefore, the evidence supports a knowledge-constrained learning-to-rank contribution with graph and hypergraph features as interpretable complementary signals, not a claim that graph structure is the dominant source of aggregate improvement.

\subsection{Hypergraph Structure Provides Modest Early-Rank Benefits}

Comparison (5) evaluates whether the n-ary hyperedge representation provides benefits over a pairwise graph decomposition that breaks each multi-node hyperedge into anchor-based pairwise edges. KCH-MedRank improves Recall@10 over pairwise graph LTR by 0.0023 and nDCG@10 by 0.0024, but these differences are not statistically significant at $k{=}10$ (Recall@10 $p{=}0.203$; nDCG@10 $p{=}0.062$, paired bootstrap). This result places an important scope constraint on the contribution: at the paper's primary evaluation depth, the hypergraph topology does not provide a statistically distinguishable advantage over a simpler pairwise graph with the same medical knowledge features. The hypergraph should therefore be understood as a structural variant within the broader knowledge-constrained learning-to-rank framework, not as a qualitatively different representational class.

At $k{=}5$, Recall@5 improves by 0.0054 ($p{=}0.003$, paired bootstrap) and nDCG@5 by 0.0037 ($p{=}0.028$), confirming a modest early-rank benefit. This suggests that the hypergraph's higher-order connectivity provides incremental value for earlier ranking positions, where the accumulated structural signal from shared entities and MeSH hierarchy paths can more readily distinguish among top-tier candidates.

\paragraph{Stratified Analysis.} To understand when the hypergraph structure matters most, we stratify the test set by the structural characteristics of each question. Figure~\ref{fig:hypergraph-vs-pairwise-stratified} presents per-stratum comparisons at $k{=}5$ and $k{=}10$.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{figures/data_figures/stratified_hypergraph_gain.pdf}
\caption{Stratified comparison of KCH-MedRank against Pairwise Graph LTR. Positive values indicate a KCH-MedRank advantage; the larger Recall@5 gains show that hypergraph effects concentrate in the early-rank region.}
\label{fig:hypergraph-vs-pairwise-stratified}
\end{figure}

The stratification reveals two key patterns. First, the hypergraph advantage is largest when the gold passage has \textit{no direct MeSH overlap} with the question ($\Delta_{\text{Recall@5}} = +0.0098$, $p=0.006$). This makes structural sense: when direct MeSH overlap exists, the pairwise graph decomposition already captures most of the alignment signal through shared-concept edges; when absent, the hypergraph's n-ary structure enables indirect paths through shared-entity clusters and MeSH hierarchy ancestors that the pairwise decomposition fragments. Second, questions with $\geq 2$ gold passages also benefit significantly from the hypergraph ($\Delta_{\text{Recall@5}} = +0.0042$, $p=0.023$), consistent with multi-evidence scenarios where the hypergraph's shared-entity hyperedges propagate ranking signal across multiple gold passages.

At $k{=}10$, no subset reaches significance, but the effect direction is consistent ($\Delta$ positive in all but one stratum). The single-gold-passage subset shows the largest nominal $\Delta$ (+0.0098), though the smaller sample ($n=204$) limits power. These results confirm that the hypergraph advantage is concentrated in the early-rank region and in cases where indirect knowledge signals compensate for missing direct concept alignment. The hypergraph is best viewed as a complementary structural component that provides targeted, modest benefits rather than a dominant architectural contribution.

\subsection{Summary of Ablation Evidence}

Taken together, the structural ablations support four conclusions. First, flat retrieval, biomedical semantic, and medical knowledge features explain most of the aggregate improvement over enhanced Hybrid RRF. Second, raw hypergraph topology is harmful without medical concept annotations. Third, medical knowledge constraints reverse the structure-induced noise inside the hypergraph variant and provide interpretable biomedical signals. Fourth, the hypergraph topology provides modest additional benefit beyond a pairwise graph mainly at $k{=}5$, with non-significant trends at $k{=}10$; the hypergraph is therefore a beneficial but non-essential structural variant, with its advantage concentrated in questions lacking direct concept overlap. The leave-one-feature-group diagnostics in the appendix complement this analysis by showing that individual knowledge feature groups are highly redundant with each other, further supporting the interpretation that medical constraints work as an integrated block rather than as independent additive components.
