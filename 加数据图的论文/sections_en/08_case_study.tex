\section{Interpretability and Case Analysis}
\label{sec:interpretability}

A key methodological claim of KCH-MedRank is that its hypergraph-based reranking is \textit{interpretable}: each gold passage rescue can be traced to specific knowledge-grounded mechanisms in the hypergraph. This section provides the quantitative evidence to support this claim.

\subsection{Three-Mechanism Classification of Rescued Passages}

We classify all 648 gold passages that KCH-MedRank rescues from outside the Hybrid RRF top-10 into the top-10 according to the dominant hypergraph mechanism:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
    \item \textbf{MeSH Hierarchy Path}: The question and the gold passage share MeSH concepts through hierarchical tree ancestor paths. For example, a question about ``DITPA'' and a passage about ``thyroid hormones'' are connected through the shared MeSH term ``thyroid hormones'' and its MeSH tree ancestors.
    \item \textbf{Shared Entity Cluster Path}: The gold passage shares biomedical entities with other candidate passages, forming shared-entity hyperedge clusters that propagate ranking signal through diffusion.
    \item \textbf{PrimeKG Relation Path}: Question entities are relationally connected to passage entities via PrimeKG biomedical relations (e.g., ``Dasatinib --treats-- Leukemia'').
\end{enumerate}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\linewidth]{figures/data_figures/interpretability_mechanisms_chart.pdf}
\caption{Dominant interpretable mechanisms among 648 rescued gold passages. Bars show the share of rescued passages assigned to each dominant mechanism; the line shows average rank gain.}
\label{fig:interpretability-mechanisms}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:interpretability-mechanisms} presents the classification results. The MeSH hierarchy path is the dominant interpretable mechanism, accounting for 75.9\% of rescues with an average rank improvement of +22.1 positions. The shared entity cluster path accounts for 23.9\% with an average gain of +14.0 positions. The PrimeKG relation path is the least frequent (0.2\%), which is expected given the sparsity of structured relations in biomedical knowledge graphs relative to the density of MeSH annotations available for all PubMed documents.

The mechanism co-occurrence analysis shows that 89.2\% of rescues involve at least two concurrent knowledge pathways, and 39.5\% activate all three mechanisms simultaneously. This confirms that the medical knowledge constraints in KCH-MedRank function as an integrated block, consistent with the structural ablation finding that removing the entire medical knowledge block causes the hypergraph to regress to noise.

Table~\ref{tab:case-study} and Figure~\ref{fig:gpt-case-study} illustrate an EGFR exon 19 NSCLC rescue. The question asks for the guideline-recommended first-line treatment for adults with non-small cell lung cancer harboring EGFR exon 19 deletions. Enhanced Hybrid RRF ranks a relevant NCCN guideline passage below the top 10, behind passages that are semantically related but less directly answer the guideline question. KCH-MedRank promotes the guideline passage to rank 1 because the passage aligns with the question through the disease entity, EGFR mutation, treatment entity osimertinib, MeSH hierarchy, and shared candidate-cluster links. This is an illustrative evidence path rather than a causal attribution result.

The lost ubiquitination case shows the opposite behavior. A gold passage drops from rank 2 to rank 18 even though it discusses mono-ubiquitinated LDH-A and poly-ubiquitinated LDH. The question and passage contain related biochemical terminology, but the synonym and hierarchy features do not fully align the surface forms and broader post-translational-processing concepts. Similar losses occur for thyroid hormone analogs, cilengitide receptors, dasatinib treatment, GIST treatment, and Fanconi anemia. These examples motivate future improvements in biomedical concept normalization rather than stronger claims about the relation features.

\subsection{Quantitative Interpretability Summary}

The three-mechanism analysis provides quantitative evidence that the hypergraph features activated by KCH-MedRank's ranking decisions follow interpretable knowledge-grounded pathways:

\begin{itemize}
    \item 100\% of rescued passages show activation of at least one knowledge feature group (zero pure diffusion-only rescues), under our heuristic classification of dominant mechanisms.
    \item The MeSH hierarchy path is the most frequently activated dominant pathway in 75.9\% of rescues, demonstrating that the hierarchical MeSH tree structure carries the strongest knowledge signal.
    \item 89.2\% of rescues involve concurrent activation of multiple knowledge pathways, confirming the complementary nature of the knowledge constraints.
    \item The average rank gain is substantially larger for MeSH-driven (+22.1) than entity-driven (+14.0) rescues, suggesting that hierarchical concept alignment provides stronger ranking signal than flat entity co-occurrence.
    \item \textbf{Limitation}: The mechanism classification uses the same feature scores that the LambdaMART ranker consumes; it identifies which knowledge features are most strongly activated, not which features causally drive the ranking. Future work with model-agnostic attribution methods (e.g., SHAP) could strengthen the causal interpretability claim.
\end{itemize}
