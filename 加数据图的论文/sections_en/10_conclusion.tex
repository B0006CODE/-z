\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented KCH-MedRank, a knowledge-constrained learning-to-rank framework for biomedical evidence retrieval and reranking. The method keeps first-stage retrieval practical, combines retrieval and biomedical semantic signals with MeSH, entity, and relation features, and optionally adds local graph or hypergraph structural features for interpretability. A flat knowledge LTR baseline and a pairwise graph decomposition serve as controlled structural baselines. The comparison reveals that flat biomedical knowledge features explain most aggregate gains, while the hypergraph provides modest early-rank benefits ($k{=}5$) but does not significantly outperform the pairwise graph or flat knowledge baseline at $k{=}10$.

The BioASQ results show stronger top-10 evidence retrieval than original and enhanced Hybrid RRF, with statistically significant improvements across all top-10 retrieval metrics against enhanced Hybrid RRF. Against a true MedCPT Cross-Encoder baseline on the same enhanced candidate pool, the strongest supported gain is statistically significant Recall@10 improvement; MRR@10 and nDCG@10 remain positive but non-significant trends. A controlled reranking-stage timing comparison further shows that KCH-MedRank uses a precomputed dense biomedical semantic feature rather than a Cross-Encoder feature and is 18.6$\times$ faster than MedCPT Cross-Encoder on the held-out test pool when candidate generation, one-time model loading, and offline training are excluded.

A structural ablation provides the central evidence for the medical knowledge contribution. The local hypergraph alone, without medical knowledge annotations, introduces structural noise and underperforms the flat knowledge LTR baseline. Adding biomedical entity features, exact and hierarchy-aware MeSH features, and auxiliary relation features reverses this effect relative to the no-medical-knowledge hypergraph variant. This demonstrates that medical knowledge constraints are necessary for local structure to be useful rather than noisy. At the same time, the full model is not significantly better than the flat knowledge LTR baseline at the primary top-10 depth, so the supported contribution is a white-box knowledge-constrained evidence selection framework with complementary structural features, not a standalone hypergraph breakthrough.

An external BEIR NFCorpus retrieval-only diagnostic supports the robustness of supervised retrieval-feature reranking, while also showing that the full medical knowledge feature stack should not be claimed to transfer without dataset-specific construction.

The appendix PubMedQA Qwen3-8B pilot indicates that evidence ordering can affect downstream answer selection under a fixed prompt and decoding setup, but it remains a limited diagnostic rather than a primary generation claim. Overall, the results support KCH-MedRank as a white-box evidence selection module for evidence-grounded medical QA. The method should not be viewed as a clinically validated answer generation or decision-support system.
