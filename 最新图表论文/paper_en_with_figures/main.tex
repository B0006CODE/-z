\title{KCH-MedRank: Knowledge-Constrained Learning-to-Rank for Biomedical Evidence Retrieval}

\begin{abstract}
Biomedical retrievers routinely place gold evidence passages inside the candidate pool and then fail to pull them into the top 10. The passages are not irrelevant. Pairwise query-passage scoring simply has no way to see the higher-order medical relations that connect them---a passage about the thyroid hormone analog DITPA and a passage about levothyroxine share almost no surface lexical overlap, yet both reside in the same Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) subtree for thyroid hormones. We introduce KCH-MedRank, a knowledge-constrained learning-to-rank framework for biomedical evidence reranking that gives the ranker access to retrieval scores, MeSH hierarchy signals, biomedical entity overlap, PrimeKG knowledge-graph relations, and an optional local hypergraph. On BioASQ, KCH-MedRank beats a true MedCPT Cross-Encoder baseline on Recall@10 ($+0.0157$, $p=0.0076$) and runs $18.6\times$ faster at reranking time (reranking-stage latency under cached features, excluding candidate generation). The central finding is that flat biomedical knowledge features explain most aggregate gains. Hypergraph structure provides targeted early-rank improvements under indirect-evidence conditions, but the full model does not significantly exceed the flat knowledge baseline at the standard top-10 evaluation depth ($\Delta{\text{Recall@10}}{=}+0.0020$, n.s.). Controlled ablation indicates that domain-grounded annotations are critical for productive graph-based reranking, although a full factorial graph-by-knowledge design would further isolate topology effects. We categorize 648 of 661 rescued passages by dominant activated knowledge mechanism---MeSH hierarchy paths account for 75.9\% of them, shared entity clusters for 23.9\%, and PrimeKG relations for the remainder. Supplementary diagnostics on PubMedQA and BEIR NFCorpus indicate that supervised reranking with retrieval features generalizes, while full knowledge-augmented generalization remains to be tested.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{5pt}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Biomedical evidence retrieval; learning-to-rank; medical knowledge graph; MeSH hierarchy; hypergraph diffusion; interpretable reranking
